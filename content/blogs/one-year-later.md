It's hard to believe it's already been a year since I landed back at Schiphol. I've been meaning to write this one for a while — partly to close out the blog properly, and partly because I needed some distance from the trip before I could actually say something honest about it.

## What Changed on Seoulo

I spent a good chunk of the last month finally finishing up this blog, so let me start with the nerdy part. A few things changed behind the scenes here on Seoulo since I got back:

- Added geo data for the last stretch of days that never got it
- A hover preview when you're browsing the day list
- A handful more blog posts (this one included)
- Migrated off Vercel and ImageKit, the site now runs fully static
- Some extra styling and filtering options throughout

None of that changes what's actually written here, but I wanted the site itself to feel finished, not just abandoned mid-build.

## What Changed in Me

- I've stopped actively studying Korean. Without a clear goal pulling me forward, it's been hard to keep the habit going the way I did before the trip.
- I haven't managed to stay in touch with most of the people I met along the way. The friends from the theme park day were great company, but it always felt a little transactional. The people from my language exchange were genuinely fun to talk to, but I never got anyone's number. I kept messaging with Katherine for a while after I got home, but we eventually drifted — it's hard to keep something like that going across a timezone and a continent.
- MAMA, of all things, is what cooled me off K-pop for a while. Seeing that much of the machinery up close left me with one recurring thought: *I am too old for this.* I still listen, I still go to concerts, and I still follow along, but I'm noticeably less invested than I used to be. New groups don't really pull me in the way they once did. And with TWICE's contract situation shifting this past year — members signing individually while staying on for group activities — it feels like the era I fell in love with is entering its final chapter.

  I'm still so glad I made it to their 10th anniversary while I was out there. ## Post-Trip Pessimism

Coming back was harder than I expected, in a way nobody really warns you about. I'd been on such a high for ten weeks, and landing back into normal life — cooking for myself again, working full time, all the mundane admin of just existing — drained me more than I thought it would. I picked up more responsibilities at work, which I used as another way to avoid sitting with how I felt, and I lost a fair amount of time to video games trying to fill the same gap. None of it really worked; the tired, flat feeling stuck around longer than I wanted to admit.

What actually pulled me out of it was going back to *doing* things: flying to Amsterdam to see TWICE live again, and a trip to the south of France with friends not long after. Both reminded me that the trip wasn't the only good thing left — I just had to go looking for the next one instead of waiting for it to come to me.

## Next Steps

There was so much left unfinished when I got back — mostly this blog — that it weighed on me more than it probably should have. Going through everything again this past month to finally wrap it up helped more than I expected; reliving it all reminded me why I wanted to write it down in the first place.

But it also made clear what's next. My personal to-do list these days looks a little different from the one I had while traveling: get my driver's license, buy a place of my own, and — maybe the hardest one to plan for — meet someone.

Thanks for following along, whether you read every single day post or just checked in for the highlights. This trip changed me more than I expected a ten-week vacation could, and having this whole blog to look back on makes that a lot easier to remember.
