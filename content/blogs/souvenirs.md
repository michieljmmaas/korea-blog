I picked up a lot of good souvenirs on this trip. Some of them are physical objects I can display on a shelf, some are things I made myself, and some are just memories I've tried to turn into something tangible. Here's a rundown of the ones I'm most proud of.

## Key Rings

As many of you know, I collect keyrings. I did my best to score a good one everywhere I went, and I ended up with a solid little collection by the end.

It started early — my first one was a small tiger keyring I picked up after a language exchange in Seoul on <Day 22>. From there it just kept growing: a slightly-too-earnest "National Congress" one from the Diet Building in Tokyo on <Day 46> (Japan's parliament, not an American one, despite what the English translation on the box suggested), and a Tall Bear keychain from Alishan that I refused to leave Taiwan without on <Day 59>. I rounded the set off with one more from Hong Kong before flying home, just to make sure every stop on the trip was represented.

## Crests

I also made a small set of crests for myself — one for each personal milestone I hit along the way. Nothing official, just something I designed to mark the moments that mattered: reaching the top of Bukhansan, surviving MAMA, hitting 70 days on the road. Turning them into something I can actually pin up is still on my list, but the designs themselves already mean a lot to me.

## Photoshoot

I count the pictures from my professional photoshoot as a souvenir too, maybe my favorite one. Three outfits, three sides of my time here: hanbok at the palace for culture, a dress shirt in the hotel lounge for the "office," and a tourist fit in the neon-soaked alleys of Euljiro for adventure. ## Collage

I kept every plane ticket, museum booklet, and show receipt I could get my hands on along the way — including the personalized booklet I put together at the *Ultra Convenience Store* exhibition on <Day 38>. I glued them all together into one big collage and hung it up at home. It's a bit chaotic to look at, but every piece on it has a story attached.

## Picture Frame

I took an enormous number of pictures on this trip, and they're all posted here on the blog — but let's be honest, I don't exactly sit down and scroll through my own blog for fun. I wanted something physical to remind me of it all instead.

My first instinct was to buy a digital picture frame, but every option I found was either too bright, needed constant charging, or ran on closed-source software I couldn't touch. So I found an open-source version online and built on top of it myself: I wrote my own display code, swapped in a bigger battery, and added a Witty Pi to stretch the battery life from about 4 hours to 4 weeks.

I also designed and 3D printed a custom frame for it, so it actually looks like it belongs on a shelf instead of a prototype on my desk. The code and the frame files are both up on GitHub if you want to build your own.

## Personal Growth

Above all the physical stuff, what I'll actually keep with me the longest is how much this trip changed me. Ten weeks ago I was too nervous to eat dinner alone (I wrote about that particular fear in <Blog worst-things desc="My Flop 10">) — by the end I was ordering live octopus off a menu I couldn't read and calling it a Tuesday.

I set out with a list of goals for this trip, and I hit almost all of them. But the bigger win was everything I didn't plan for: getting comfortable being alone in a foreign country, holding a full conversation in Korean I'd only ever practiced in a classroom, and figuring out how to stay professional and deliver for work while living completely out of a suitcase. None of that fits in a keyring, but it's the souvenir I'm proudest of.
