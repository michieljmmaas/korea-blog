Alishan was the last big adventure of my Taiwan leg, and it took some real effort to get to — a train, a very specific 2.5-hour bus, and a whole day I could only spend about 24 hours in. Worth every bit of the hassle. This is mostly a photo dump, but here's the rundown of what I actually saw up there. Full day-by-day version here: <Day 59> and <Day 60>.

## Getting There

Getting up the mountain is its own little adventure. Between a train ticket that vanished without a confirmation email and a bus booking site entirely in Mandarin, I was fully winging it until I realized halfway through the train ride that I could just book through Klook. Once the bus started climbing, the winding roads and stuffy cabin meant I spent most of the ride with my eyes closed rather than looking at the view — but stepping off into the cool mountain air at the top made up for all of it instantly.

<Img arrival desc="Stepping off the bus into actual fresh air">

Check-in came with a map of the park that looked like absolute chaos — paths, landmarks, and decoration all blending into one another with no obvious logic. It took a proper look around in person before any of it actually made sense.

<Img map desc="I mean, look at this thing!">

## Two Sisters Pond & First Impressions

With the park closing at sundown, I had about an hour and a half to explore before dark. Somehow that was enough — a small temple, Two Sisters Pond, and a couple of viewpoints, all connected by a nice mix of paved walkways and forest trail.

## The Sunrise Hike

Sunrise was scheduled for 6:40, with the little mountain train leaving at 5:20 — so the alarm went off at 4:15. Good thing it did, because the platform was already packed by the time I got there. The train itself was tiny, dark, and adorable.

<Img sunrise-train desc="Choo choo!">

At the summit, the sky was already glowing but the peaks were still blocking the sun itself, so it was a 40-minute wait in about 10°C weather (well-prepared with layers, and something a little stronger than coffee to keep warm). Watching the sun finally break free over the mountains — without a phone screen in the way — was one of those moments that's genuinely hard to describe. It hit harder than I expected.

<Img sunrise desc="Peekaboo!">

## The Ancient Trees

Once the crowds thinned out, I hiked back down through the forest at my own pace — exactly the kind of park I love: lush, dense, mossy, and dotted with genuinely ancient trees along a scenic little railroad. A few standouts:

- The biggest tree in the whole park
- A stump that looks uncannily like a woolly mammoth
- The famous Three Generation Tree — three trees that grew stacked directly on top of each other over the centuries

## Worth It

Less than 24 hours total, and it still left one of the biggest impressions of the whole trip. The forest, the air, the sunrise — all of it. Easily one of my favorite hikes, full stop: <Blog best-things desc="My Top 10">.
