I wanted to go to MAMA for years. Clips from previous editions always looked so cool online — the stages, the collabs, the sheer scale of it. So when I found out it would be held in Hong Kong this year, right in the middle of my trip, it felt like fate. I ended up going for both days.

Getting there live was… different from the clips. Don't get me wrong, sitting in a real arena with tens of thousands of other fans is still a spectacle. But I didn't connect with a lot of the acts on the lineup, and the whole thing runs less like a concert and more like an award show with performances stitched in between.

<Img crowd desc="50,000 of my closest friends">

## Getting the Tickets Was a Whole Ordeal

I booked my flight and hotel for Hong Kong before MAMA had even announced its dates or venue — I was just hoping it would line up. When it finally went on sale, I sat at my laptop ready to book, only for the site to buckle under the load the second tickets dropped. By the time I got through, everything in my section was gone.

So I had to go through a resale platform instead, paying a hefty premium just to guarantee a seat for both days. I wrote more about that whole saga (and the two weeks of radio silence that followed) in <Blog worst-things desc="My Flop 10">. Let's just say the tickets arriving at my hotel front desk on <Day 63> felt like a small miracle.

<Img tickets desc="The scalpers came through, eventually">

## The Lightsticks

The thing that impressed me most wasn't even on stage — it was in my hand. Every seat came with a free lightstick that lit up in sync with the section you were sitting in, no pairing, no app, nothing. I spent an embarrassing amount of time trying to reverse-engineer how it worked before I found the answer printed on the bag it came in. Once I figured it out I was almost more excited about that than the next performance.

<Img lightsticks desc="An entire stadium doing the wave, but with light">

## My Favorite Acts

Two full days of performances is a lot to sit through when you don't recognize half the lineup, but a few acts made the whole thing worth it:

- **SUPER JUNIOR** — Twenty-plus years in and they still know exactly how to work a crowd. This was the moment the whole stadium got on its feet, ad-break fatigue and all.
- **AESPA** — Not my favorite releases of theirs this year, but live is a completely different story. Winter picking up a guitar mid-performance was a genuinely unfair thing to do to the crowd.
- **MEOVV** — A group that debuted less than two years ago, going toe-to-toe with acts that have been doing this for decades. They earned every bit of that reaction.

Honorable mention to G-DRAGON, if only for how much of the arena was visibly there just for him.

## The (Slightly Ridiculous) Awards

MAMA hands out a *lot* of trophies, and by the second day I started paying closer attention to the categories themselves rather than the performances. Some of my favorites, pulled straight from the program:

- **Global Trend Song** — because someone had to win something and AESPA had already swept everything else
- **Inspiring Achievement** — awarded, as far as I can tell, mostly for showing up
- **Music Visionary of the Year** — went to *KPop Demon Hunters*, because apparently that needed a trophy too
- **Favorite Asian Artist** — went to a Japanese group, which raised more questions than it answered

There's also a running joke that the stage only fits two or three artists comfortably, and somehow it's always the artists already sitting on stage who win the next award. Lucky seating, I guess.

<Img trophy desc="Acting surprised is apparently part of the choreography">

## So, Was It Worth It?

Honestly? Mixed. The scale of it is unlike anything else I've been to, and I'm glad I can say I've experienced it live. But between the price of the tickets, the acts I didn't know, and the vanity of the awards themselves, I don't think I'd fly back specifically for it again.
