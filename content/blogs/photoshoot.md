Somewhere in the middle of planning this trip, I decided I wanted more than just my own phone pictures to remember it by. So I booked a professional photoshoot — and, more importantly, a photographer patient enough to drag me around Seoul for an entire day.

## Finding a Photographer

A fellow digital nomad recommended [Jazmin](https://www.instagram.com/jazzinseoul_snap/), and after a few messages back and forth on Instagram, we set a date. I had a rough plan going in: I wanted to capture three sides of my time in Seoul — the culture I'd immersed myself in, the remote work life I'd been building, and the more adventurous side of just exploring the city. Three outfits, three locations, one very long day.

I wrote about the day itself as it happened on <Day 51>, but I wanted to give the final results their own space here — mostly so I have somewhere to actually dump all the edited photos.

## Korean Culture — Hanbok at Gyeongbokgung

Renting a hanbok and taking pictures around one of Seoul's palaces is about as classic a Seoul activity as it gets — you even get free palace entry if you're wearing one. I picked a style I liked at the rental shop near Jazmin's usual spot, and after a bit of trouble finding shoes in my size, we were off.

I was stiff for the first few shots — my cheeks were already sore from smiling before we'd even started — but we found a rhythm after a while, and the palace grounds did most of the heavy lifting.

## Working Remotely — Dress Shirt in the Office

For the "work" set, we settled on the lounge at my hotel — great views, and the most honest depiction of what my day-to-day actually looked like out here. We started later than planned, which turned out to be a lucky break: we caught the sunset and the skyline lighting up right on cue.

Jazmin told me to "pretend to work." I ended up actually working on this blog instead, which made the whole thing feel a lot less staged.

## Adventuring — Neon Streets of Euljiro

The last set was the hardest. By this point we were both running on fumes, and Euljiro's narrow alleys and constant foot traffic made it tricky to grab a clean shot. But it's exactly the atmosphere I wanted — tiny restaurants, crowds, and enough neon to make the whole thing feel like a different city entirely.

## The Results

The raw shots came back a few days later, and — as it turns out — being a good model is a lot harder than it looks. Half of them had me squinting into the sun, mid-blink, or standing like I'd never used my own limbs before. It's a strange thing, seeing yourself the way someone else's camera sees you; a mirror only ever shows you one angle.

Picking favorites for editing was brutal. I ended up with far more keepers than I expected, and I'm genuinely thrilled with how the final set turned out — enough that I made a collage out of some of them and hung it up above my desk.
